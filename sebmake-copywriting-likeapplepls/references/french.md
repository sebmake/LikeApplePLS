# Writing it in French

The register was measured in English on 8 keynotes, and in French on the official French subtitles of the 4 most recent ones (2024 to 2026 : 3 008 sentences, 50 100 words ; medians in `targets.json`). The table below details the two September keynotes (2025, 2026) ; across the four, the median is 15.5 words per sentence, 22 % at 8 words or fewer, 18 % at 25 or more, *vous* 3.7 and *nous* 1.1 per 100 words, *Et* opens 10 % of sentences, *C'est* 3.4 %, *Voici* 1.1 %. The rest is the English rule carried over, or a convention of French usage.

## 1. What the French corpus does

| Measure | FR 2025 | FR 2026 | What it means for a rewrite |
|---|---|---|---|
| words per sentence, median | 14 | 17 | French sentences run 1 to 3 words longer than the English originals (14 and 15) : the same idea, more syllables. Don't fight it by cutting sense ; cut words. |
| sentences ≤ 8 words | 23 % | 19 % | one short sentence in five, where English has one in four. Keep them : the short sentence is what lands. |
| sentences ≥ 25 words | 14 % | 23 % | the 2026 translation drifts long. Target the 2025 figure. |
| *vous* per 100 words | 1,6 | 3,1 | the reader is *vous*, every few sentences |
| *tu* | 0,04 | 0,02 | never, on stage. In writing to one person, *tu* is the brand's choice (ask). |
| *nous / notre* | 1,6 | 1,05 | the company speaks as *nous* on stage |
| *on* | 0,08 | 0,12 | almost absent in the official register ; the casual written register uses it (convention : *on* for a brand that says *tu*) |
| *je* | 0,56 | 0,14 | a signed verdict, rarely |
| sentences opening with *Et* | 8,9 % | 9,5 % | the thread opener survives translation (English *And* : 12 %). Keep it in a script, drop it in print. |
| sentences opening with *Voici* | 1,2 % | 1,4 % | *Voici* carries three English moves : announcing, revealing, handing over. Vary it. |
| sentences opening with *Vous* | 1,2 % | 5,5 % | the 2026 register turns toward the reader from the first word |
| *pouvez / peut* per 100 sentences | 2,6 | 10,4 | « vous pouvez » is the French benefit turn ; English says « so you can » |
| future (simple or *aller*) | 2,3 % | 1,7 % | present tense ; the future for a date only |
| questions | 0,2 % | 0,9 % | almost none |
| exclamation marks | 1,5 % | 1,2 % | fewer than in English (3 to 5 %, applause lines) : in print, none |
| superlative adjectives (*incroyable, puissant, remarquable…*) | ≈ 8 % of sentences | ≈ 6 % | one per announcement |
| *le / la / les plus* | 6,7 % | 6,3 % | the relative superlative is the French form of « -est » |
| *à ce jour* | 2,6 % | 1,2 % | the bound of « yet » |
| *jamais* (« que jamais », « jamais vu ») | 1,5 % | 3,4 % | the bound of « ever » |
| *au monde* | 1,5 % | 0,6 % | absolute : only with proof |
| *jusqu'à* | 3,8 % (2026) | | « up to » : always with its condition |
| sentences with a figure | 27 % | 22 % | one sentence in four carries a number |
| triads (A, B et C) | 7 % | 10 % | fewer than in English (14 %) : French lists in twos as often as in threes |
| *vraiment / très / tellement* | 2,5 % | 2,1 % | intensifiers stay rare |
| *il suffit* | 0,3 % | 0,8 % | the French « just » : « il suffit de [geste] » |
| *tout ça / tout cela* | 0,3 % | 1,7 % | the « all in / all with » wrap : « tout ça, dans [objet] » |

## 2. The French turns (templates, not quotes)

| Move | EN | FR | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| announce | Introducing [Name]. | Voici [Nom]. | then vary : « [Nom] arrive. », « Découvrez [Nom]. » (on screen) |
| verdict | [Name] is the [simple] way to [benefit]. | [Nom], c'est le moyen [le plus simple] de [bénéfice]. | the comma + « c'est » is spoken French ; in print : « [Nom] est… » |
| benefit turn | so you can [verb] | pour [verbe] / vous pouvez donc [verbe] / de quoi [verbe] | « de quoi » turns a figure into a use |
| bound superlative | our most [adj] [X] yet | notre [X] le plus [adj] à ce jour | « jamais » for software : « plus [adj] que jamais » |
| first | for the first time | pour la première fois | add « chez nous » if in doubt |
| figure | [N] times [adj-er] than [base] | [N] fois plus [adj] que [base] | base in the same sentence |
| up to | up to [N] [unit] | jusqu'à [N] [unité] | condition in the same sentence : « jusqu'à [N], selon [condition] » |
| just | just [verb] | il suffit de [verbe] | not « simplement » every time |
| wrap | all in [X] | tout ça, dans [X] / le tout, dans [X] | |
| price | starts at [price] | à partir de [prix] | number before the symbol, non-breaking space : 1 099 € ; HT or TTC said |
| availability | available [date] | disponible le [date] | « le 12 juin », never an ordinal except « le 1er » |
| ask (screen) | Order today | Commandez dès aujourd'hui | imperative on screen ; « vous pouvez commander » aloud |
| ask (tu) | Try it | Essaie | only if the brand says *tu* everywhere |
| recap | So that's [X]. | Voilà pour [X]. | |
| close | We can't wait for you to [verb]. | Nous avons hâte que vous [verbe]. / On a hâte que vous [verbe]. | « hâte » rather than « impatients » |

## 3. Register : vous, tu, on

- **Vous** is the default to a reader, as in the corpus (4 of 4 French keynotes). It is asked, never guessed, when the brand might say *tu*.
- **Tu** in writing only when the brand uses it everywhere (interface, emails, site). Then *on* replaces *nous* : « On a ajouté… ». Never mix *tu* and *nous* in the same text.
- **Vous** with *nous* is the formal pair ; **vous** with *on* is the warm pair (convention : most French SaaS brands). **Tu** with *on* is the casual pair.
- **Je** : a message signed by one person, about what that person did. Never for the company's opinion : « Je pense que c'est incroyable » becomes « C'est remarquable » (the personal opinion fades in French, 7 of the keynote rules).
- **Between colleagues** (a script with several speakers) : *tu* unless the team says *vous* every day.

## 4. Typography and format

- Non-breaking space before « : ; ! ? » and « % », inside « » quotes.
- Decimal comma (1,5), thin or non-breaking space for thousands (12 000).
- Dates : « le 12 juin 2026 » ; times : « 9 h 41 » ; durations : « 2 h 30 ».
- Currency after the number : « 29 € HT / mois ». Units metric ; screen sizes stay in inches.
- Capitals : sentence case for titles and buttons (« Télécharger la facture », not « Télécharger La Facture »). Product names keep their own spelling.
- Accents on capitals (« À », « É »).
- No English word where a French one exists and is used by the audience : *tableau de bord* rather than *dashboard* in text for the general public ; the product team's own English terms are kept only if the readers use them (ask).
- Gender : paired forms aloud (« celles et ceux »), a neutral word in print rather than a midpoint ; the brand's style guide wins.

## 5. Length

French is longer : after rewriting, recount. A slide keeps 10 words at most **in French** ; a button keeps its width ; an email keeps 60 to 180 words. When the French sentence passes 25 words, split it rather than shrinking the idea.

## 6. What to keep from English, what to drop

- Keep : the present tense, *vous*, the verdict sentence, the bound superlative, the figure with its base, the verb-first ask, the benefit turn.
- Drop : « Nous sommes ravis de vous annoncer » (0 in the FR corpus for « ravis »), « N'hésitez pas », « Veuillez », « dans le cadre de », « afin de », « permettre de », « mise en place », « au niveau de », « en termes de », « problématique », « solution » : each hides a plain verb or noun. The checker flags them (`check_copy.py`, W1 / U3).
